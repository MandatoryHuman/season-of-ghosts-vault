![[Komen.webp|float-left|400]]Somboon is a [[Sudden Ghost|Phi Tai Hong]] who [[Otonashi Amaya|Amaya]] encountered many years ago. It was a bitterly cold and damp Autumn evening as the scent of copper and stagnant rainwater hung heavy in the air where the old crossroads met the forest edge. Amaya knelt in the mud, her fingers tracing the deep, jagged gouges in the earth. A violent end had occurred here and - for whatever reason - had chosen this day to resurface.

The temperature plummeted, freezing the breath in her lungs. From the shadows of the treeline, Somboon materialised. His lower half was nothing but tattered, swirling cyan mist, anchoring him to the exact spot where his life had been brutally cut short. A void existed where his sternum should have been. The edges of the exposed thoracic cavity were ragged and warped, endlessly weeping a torrent of blood that vanished before it hit the ground.

Somboon threw his head back, his hollow, pitch-black eyes wide as he released a soundless, agonising shriek. Even this early in her career, Amaya knew that a Phi Tai Hong did not reason. They were born of sudden, traumatic death, their consciousness shattered by the sheer violence of their final moments. Somboon lunged forward, his hands twisted into rigid claws, driven entirely by the endless loop of his own agony.

Amaya sidestepped the desperate swipe, the unnatural cold of his ethereal form biting through her cloak. A more foolish exorcist might have tried to blast the spirit apart with radiant light, but Amaya was all too aware that destroying a traumatised soul only scattered the fragments, leaving the area blighted. To unravel a Phi Tai Hong, one has to address the anchor.

While the ghost readied for another frenzied attack, Amaya reached into her component pouch and withdrew a pinch of grave dust and dried nightshade. As Somboon lunged again, Amaya stepped into his claws which dug deep into her midriff, creating an injury that [[Damihansig Mababangloob|Dami]] would be treating for weeks. Nevertheless, the position gave Amaya just the right angle to thrust her glowing, ash-covered hand right toward the epicentre of the massive, haemorrhaging wound in the spirit's chest.

The effect was instantaneous. Somboon froze; for the first time since his murder, the pain stopped. The violent torrent of crimson spilling from his chest slowed to a trickle, the ghostly blood losing its brilliant hue as the trauma detached from his soul.

"Go," Amaya whispered, withdrawing her hand. "The pain is done." Somboon closed his eyes, and the tattered cyan mist holding him together finally unravelled. He dissolved into a fine, pale vapour that drifted upwards, carried away by the autumn breeze. Amaya stood alone at the crossroads, clutching at her own wound as the forest returned to a peaceful, natural quiet.
